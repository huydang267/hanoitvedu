COMMON MISTAKES WHEN USING AI FOR PLANNING

TIP FOR EDITORIAL TEAMS
Avoid these mistakes to make AI a reliable
partner in your editorial planning process, not a
shortcut that leads to incomplete or risky stories.

1

Accepting AI’s first set of
questions without editing

AI doesn’t know who your readers are, or what your
newsroom has already published on the topic. Always
review the question list and drop anything already
answered or irrelevant to your angle.

2

Stopping after one round
of Q&A

A solid research plan usually needs 2-3 rounds with AI:
ask → review results → ask again to dig deeper or narrow
scope. Don’t treat the first answer as the final plan.

3

Using overly generic
keywords

“Hanoi electric bus” will surface thousands of results
not specifically about the new route. The more specific
(route name, timeframe, agency name), the more
reliable the search results.

4

Skipping the gap-check
step due to time pressure

This is the most common and riskiest mistake — it leads
to publishing stories missing key information that a
single question could have caught early.

5

Confusing “AI asks good
questions” with “AI gives
correct answers”

AI is good at suggesting what to ask, but not reliable
when it answers those questions itself. Keep these two
things clearly separated in your workflow.

KEY IDEA
KEY IDEA

Use AI to ask smarter questions, not to replace critical thinking.
